PALEOMAGNETIC RECORDS FROM PULSED MAGMATISM → SWLLIP & CARDENAS BASALT

PUBLICATION

→ PALEOMAGNETISM & GEOCHRONOLOGY

• Co-Author: Nicolas S Anderson
• Lead Author: Yiming Zhang
• Additional Authors: Michael T Mohr, Lyle L Nelson, Francis A Macdonald, Mark D Schmitz, Olivia G Thurston, William R Guenthner, Karl E Karlstrom, Nicholas L Swanson‐Hysell

Abstract

Mafic intrusions, lava flows, and felsic plutons in southwestern Laurentia have been hypothesized to be associated with the emplacement of a late Mesoproterozoic (Stenian Period) large igneous province. Improved geochronologic data resolve distinct episodes of mafic magmatism in the region.

The ca. 1,098 Ma main pulse of southwestern Laurentia large igneous province (SWLLIP) magmatism is recorded by mafic intrusions across southeastern California to central Arizona. A younger episode of volcanism resulted in eruptions that formed the ca. 1,082 Ma Cardenas Basalt, which is the uppermost unit of the Unkar Group in the Grand Canyon.

With the updated geochronological constraints, we develop new paleomagnetic data from mafic sills in the SWLLIP. These paleomagnetic records from pulsed magmatism in the southwestern Laurentia large igneous province and Cardenas Basalt support rapid late Mesoproterozoic plate motion.

This research represents a significant contribution to understanding the dynamics of late Mesoproterozoic plate tectonics and large igneous province emplacement. The work combines high-precision geochronology with paleomagnetic analysis to constrain the timing and tectonic significance of SWLLIP magmatism.
