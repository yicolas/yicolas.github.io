Abstract

Paleomagnetism & Geochronology of Mesoproterozoic Diabase Sills from Death Valley, California: Evaluating the Bifurcating Plume Hypothesis for the Southwestern Laurentia large igneous province.

This research investigates the Mesoproterozoic diabase sills in Death Valley, California, to test the bifurcating plume hypothesis for the Southwestern Laurentia Large Igneous Province (SWLLIP). Using paleomagnetic analysis and U-Pb geochronology, the study examines whether these intrusions represent a distinct pulse of magmatism related to a proposed mantle plume bifurcation event approximately 1.1 billion years ago.

The work involved extensive field sampling, paleomagnetic measurements on a SQUID magnetometer, and geochronological analysis. Results contribute to understanding Late Mesoproterozoic plate tectonics and the dynamics of large igneous province emplacement. The research was presented at the 2022 GSA Joint Section Meeting in Las Vegas and contributed to subsequent publications in high-impact journals.

This thesis was recognized with High Honors and contributed to my acceptance into the Caltech Geology PhD program, where I continue to investigate fundamental questions in Earth's deep-time geology using paleomagnetism, geochronology, and field-based approaches.
