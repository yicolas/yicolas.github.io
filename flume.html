§3. Inundation equations

Sea level equivalent

SLE = Vice × ρiceAocean × ρsw − Vbelow SLAocean
Vice = ice sheet volume (km³) · ρice = 917 kg/m³ · Aocean = ocean surface area (km²) · ρsw = 1028 kg/m³ · Vbelow SL = ice grounded below sea level

Linear inundation reach

xreach = ΔSLSlinear where Slinear = htargetL
xreach = inland flooding distance (km) · ΔSL = eustatic sea level rise (m) · Slinear = constant slope from coast to target · htarget = target city elevation (m) · L = coast-to-target distance (km)

Concave (piecewise) inundation

xreach = max { d : e(d) ≤ ΔSL }
e(d) = piecewise-linear elevation profile interpolated between waypoints · Sea penetrates to the farthest point where riverbed elevation ≤ sea level

Required post-depositional uplift

U = hmodern − ΔSL
Rate = U / tage
U = total uplift since deposition (m) · hmodern = present-day elevation (m) · ΔSL = Pliocene eustatic SL (m) · tage = age of deposit (Ma)

§4. Flexural basin equations

Broken plate elastic deflection

w(x) = w₀ · e−x/α · cos(x/α)
w(x) = deflection at distance x from load (m) · w₀ = maximum deflection at thrust front (m) · x = distance from thrust front (km) · α = flexural parameter (km)

Flexural parameter

α = ⁴√( 4D / (Δρ · g) )
D = flexural rigidity (N·m) · Δρ = ρmantle − ρfill (kg/m³) · g = 9.81 m/s²

Flexural rigidity

D = E · Te³12(1−ν²)
E = Young's modulus (Pa) · Te = effective elastic thickness (m) · ν = Poisson's ratio (dimensionless)

§5. Basal shear equations

Gaussian crustal thickening

Δhc(x) = Δhmax · exp(−(x−x₀)²/2σ²)
Δhc = crustal thickening at position x (km) · Δhmax = maximum crustal thickening at arch crest (km) · x₀ = arch center distance from thrust front (km) · σ = Gaussian width of thickening dome (km)

Airy isostatic surface uplift

hsurf = Δhc × ρm − ρcρm
hsurf = surface uplift (m) · ρm = mantle density (kg/m³) · ρc = crustal density (kg/m³) · Isostatic fraction ≈ 0.182 for standard densities
Refs: Beaumont 1981 · Turcotte & Schubert 2002 · Bishop et al. 2018 · Espurt et al. 2007/10 · Regard et al. 2009 · Pérez-Gussinyé et al. 2007 · Parra et al.

§7. Sulfate budget & pyrite

Why pyrite doesn't require marine water:
Pyrite (FeS₂) forms wherever bacterial sulfate reduction occurs in anoxic conditions: SO₄²⁻ + 2CH₂O → H₂S + 2HCO₃⁻, then Fe²⁺ + H₂S → FeS₂.

Sulfate sources in the Madre de Dios foreland:
• Yecua Fm evaporites (14–7 Ma) — gypsum/anhydrite in Bolivian sub-Andean zone, actively weathering into modern rivers
• Ipururo Fm — evaporitic facies in Peruvian sub-Andean zone
• Modern Andean rivers — SO₄²⁻ = 50–200 mg/L (Stallard & Edmond 1983), vs. seawater ~2,700 mg/L
• Even 50 mg/L SO₄²⁻ is sufficient for bacterial sulfate reduction in organic-rich wetlands

Salinity without marine water:
Parra's estimated 2–16 ppt could result from evaporative concentration in floodplain lakes receiving sulfate-rich Andean runoff — a common process in modern Pantanal and Llanos de Moxos wetlands. No ocean connection required.

§9. Isotopic constraints

Geochemical tests that could confirm or refute marine influence — and what Parra lacks.

⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr — the definitive test:
• Pliocene seawater: 0.7090–0.7092
• Continental water: 0.710–0.730 (radiogenic from old crust)
• Pebas forams: 0.7090–0.7110 — continental, not marine
• Parra: NO Sr isotope data

δ¹⁸O / δ¹³C:
• Marine carbonates: δ¹⁸O ≈ 0 ‰, δ¹³C ≈ 0 to +2 ‰
• Freshwater: δ¹⁸O ≈ −2 to −12 ‰, δ¹³C ≈ −5 to −15 ‰
• Parra: NO stable isotope data

δ³⁴S on pyrite:
• Marine sulfate: δ³⁴S ≈ +10 to +25 ‰
• Evaporite-derived: δ³⁴S ≈ +10 to +20 ‰
• Freshwater bacterial: δ³⁴S ≈ −40 to +5 ‰
• Parra: NO sulfur isotope data

The absence of ANY geochemical data is a fundamental gap. Standard practice requires at minimum Sr isotopes on foram tests.

⚠ Cratonic shield caveat: Pre-uplift reconstruction applies only to Fitzcarrald Arch. The divide is Precambrian shield (Guaporé Shield, ~2000–1300 Ma, Tₑ >70 km; Pérez-Gussinyé 2007) — too rigid for Neogene deformation.

Subtracts modeled Neogene uplift (§18 flexural + §19 basal shear) from modern elevations to reconstruct the pre-uplift landscape, then tests whether Pliocene SL could reach the target along the full route. The green line is the reconstructed surface at 3.45 Ma — the actual terrain an observer would have walked on. Where the foredeep goes structurally deep (dashed brown line), sediment fill layers show how the basin was filled to near sea level by Cretaceous–Miocene deposits (3–4 km total; Horton 1997, Hermoza 2005). Layer proportions from published MdD foredeep stratigraphy: Paleozoic–Cretaceous 30%, Paleogene 15%, Miocene 45% (Ipururo/Madre de Dios Fm.; Moizinho et al. 2023), Plio-Pleistocene 10% (Campbell et al. 2006). The Fill floor parameter (§13) controls the surface elevation of this sediment fill: 0 m = fully filled to sea level (overfilled foreland); negative values = slight lacustrine/wetland depression; the actual value is uncertain and worth exploring.
